Comfort Food Corner

Come on in and sit down! These are the recipes I have been making for my family for 40 years, typed up by my grandson so they do not get lost when I am gone (not for a while yet, I hope).

Recipes

Grandma Dot's Sunday Pot Roast
Serves 6

  • 3 lb chuck roast
  • 6 carrots
  • 6 potatoes
  • 2 onions
  • 1 packet onion soup mix
  • 2 cups water
  • Salt & pepper

Brown the roast in a hot Dutch oven. Pile the vegetables around it, sprinkle the soup mix over everything, add the water, cover, and bake at 325 for 3 hours while you are at church. Gravy: whatever is in the pan plus a spoonful of flour.

Blue Ribbon Snickerdoodles
Serves 4 dozen

  • 1 cup butter
  • 1 1/2 cups sugar
  • 2 eggs
  • 2 3/4 cups flour
  • 2 tsp cream of tartar
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • Cinnamon sugar for rolling

Cream the butter and sugar, beat in the eggs, stir in the dry stuff. Roll into balls, roll the balls in cinnamon sugar, bake at 400 for 8 to 10 minutes. Won first place at the 1994 county fair.

Tater Tot Hotdish
Serves 8

  • 1 lb ground beef
  • 1 onion
  • 1 can cream of mushroom soup
  • 1 can green beans
  • 1 bag tater tots
  • 1 cup shredded cheese

Brown the beef and onion, spread in a 9x13 pan. Layer the soup, then the beans, then the cheese, then line up the tots in neat rows (this is important). Bake at 375 for 45 minutes.

Monster Cookies
Serves 5 dozen

  • 1 cup peanut butter
  • 1/2 cup butter
  • 1 cup brown sugar
  • 3 eggs
  • 4 1/2 cups oats
  • 1 cup chocolate chips
  • 1 cup candy-coated chocolates

Mix it all in your biggest bowl (there is no flour!). Drop big spoonfuls on a sheet, bake at 350 for 12 minutes. The kids will call them monster cookies. You will call them breakfast.

Church Supper Baked Beans
Serves 20

  • 4 cans pork and beans
  • 1 lb bacon
  • 1 onion
  • 1 cup brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup ketchup
  • 2 tbsp mustard

Fry the bacon and onion. Mix everything in a big roaster. Bake at 325 for 2 hours. Bring the roaster home empty.
