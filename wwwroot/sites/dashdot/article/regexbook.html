Review: Regular Expressions for Irregular People
Posted by Rotundo on Thursday October 14, @07:00PM EDT
from the s/confusion/clarity/g dept.
Gladys Okonkwo-Pratt's new book from Trout & Turtle Press promises to teach regular expressions to people who have previously only used them by copying lines out of other people's Perl scripts and praying. Our reviewer is one of those people. Read on to see if it worked.

authorGladys Okonkwo-Pratt
pages312
publisherTrout & Turtle Press
rating8/10
summaryFinally, an explanation of backtracking a human can follow.

The Scoop
The book starts with grep and ends with a full chapter on why you should not parse HTML with regular expressions, which the author then proceeds to do for 40 pages anyway, very entertainingly.

What's Good?
The diagrams of the matching engine stepping through a pattern are worth the cover price. Each chapter ends with exercises that start easy and end with patterns that look like line noise but actually work.

What's Bad?
The index is thin, and the chapter on Unicode is two pages long, one of which is a picture of a confused cat.
