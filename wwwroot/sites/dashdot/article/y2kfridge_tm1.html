Y2K Bug Found in Smart Refrigerator Firmware
Posted by CmdrGrits on Monday October 18, @09:42AM EDT
from the it's-the-y2k-dept dept.
frostbyte writes "Owners of the Kelvinator-Plus NetChill 2000, the fridge that e-mails you when you're out of milk, have discovered that it thinks January 1st 2000 is January 1st 1900. Since milk bought in 1999 will then be 99 years in the future, the fridge has decided all of it has expired and is scheduling a self-defrost. The vendor says a patch will ship on floppy disk 'well before the holidays.'" Nothing says the future like a refrigerator that needs a firmware upgrade. I'm keeping my milk in a cooler.

The NetChill 2000 runs an embedded 8-bit controller and stores the year in two BCD digits, according to a reader who dumped the EEPROM with a homemade programmer and a lot of patience. The good news: the fridge does not actually control the compressor from the clock. The bad news: it controls the ice maker, the door alarm, and the e-mail notification feature, which will inform you once per minute that your 1999 butter is a century old.

Kelvinator-Plus says customers can call a toll-free number to request the update floppy, which requires a PC with a serial port and "approximately one weekend" to apply.
